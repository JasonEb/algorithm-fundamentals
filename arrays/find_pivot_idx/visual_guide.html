Find Pivot Index

Not a two-pointer problem, for once — the trick here is realizing you never have to compute the right side at all, because it's always just leftover from the total.

  • Strictly left and strictly right. The pivot index itself is excluded from both sums.
  • Edges count as zero. If the pivot is at index 0, the left sum is 0 — same idea at the last index.
  • Return the leftmost one. If more than one index qualifies, take the first.
  • Return -1 if none exist. Not every array has a pivot.

The trap

Why re-summing both sides is O(n²)

The obvious move: for every index, add up everything to its left, add up everything to its right, and compare. It works, but each of those sums touches up to n elements, and you do it n times — O(n²).

The optimal version computes the total sum exactly once. After that, the right side of any index is just algebra — total - left_sum - nums[i] — never re-summed from scratch.

Re-sum both sides, every index

O(n²) time

Left and right sums recomputed from scratch at every index.

Total once, running left sum

O(n) time · O(1) space

One pass to total, one pass to check — the right sum is just arithmetic.

Mnemonic: "Total, minus left, minus me." That's the entire right-hand side of the check — never a real sum, just what's left over once you subtract what you already know.
